MLA Final Project Instructions 
Project overview 
Choose a topic of interest and develop a machine learning project that follows the complete ML workflow: 
problem formulation, data collection and preparation, exploratory analysis, model development, evaluation, 
and communication of results. 
You will submit a reproducible implementation/code and a slide deck, then present and defend your project. 
Your work should demonstrate both practical implementation skills and an understanding of the algorithms 
and decisions behind your solution. 
At least one model must be a neural network implemented and trained or fine-tuned using 
TensorFlow/Keras or PyTorch. 
Simply importing the library or using a pretrained model only for inference does not satisfy this requirement. 
You can do a project in a group of 3 students max 
1 Choose and define your problem 
You may choose any topic, such as healthcare, education, finance, sports, environmental science, computer 
vision, natural language processing, or recommendation systems. 
Your proposal should identify: 
 The problem you want to solve and why it matters. 
 The machine learning task, such as classification, regression, clustering, or forecasting. 
 The dataset you plan to use and its source. 
 Your intended inputs and outputs. 
 How you will measure success. 
Choose a scope that is feasible with the available time, data, and computing resources. Projects involving 
unsupervised learning or other nonstandard tasks must still include a meaningful neural network component 
and an appropriate evaluation strategy. 
2 Obtain and understand the data 
Use a public dataset or combine suitable sources. Avoid the use of synthetic data 
Document the dataset’s origin, size, features, target variable where applicable, and any usage restrictions. 
Conduct exploratory data analysis to examine distributions, missing values, outliers, class balance, and 
relationships relevant to your problem. 
Explain how your observations influence your modeling decisions. Include relevant visualizations rather than 
plots without interpretation. 
Discuss potential limitations, including sampling bias, privacy concerns, and whether the data represent the 
intended use case.

3 Prepare the data and design the evaluation 
Develop a preprocessing pipeline appropriate to your task. This may include: 
 Handling missing values, duplicates, and outliers. 
 Encoding categorical variables and scaling numerical features. 
 Preparing text, images, or time-series data. 
 Engineering or selecting features. 
 Addressing class imbalance, where relevant. 
Use an appropriate training, validation, and test strategy. Explain your choices. For time-dependent or 
grouped observations, use splits that respect those structures. 
Prevent data leakage: fit learned preprocessing steps using training data only. Use validation data or cross-
validation for model selection, and reserve the test set for the final evaluation. 
4 Develop and compare models 
Your project must include: 
1. A simple baseline, such as majority-class prediction, mean prediction, or another task-appropriate 
reference. 
2. At least one non-neural machine learning model, such as logistic regression, a decision tree, random 
forest, support vector machine, or gradient boosting. 
3. At least one neural network built with TensorFlow/Keras or PyTorch. 
For the neural network, explain the architecture, activation functions, loss function, optimizer, batch size, 
number of epochs, and any regularization techniques.  
Tune relevant hyperparameters using a documented strategy. Compare models using consistent evaluation 
procedures and describe any differences in their inputs or preprocessing. 
5 Evaluate and interpret the results 
Select metrics that fit your task and explain why they are appropriate. Do not rely on accuracy alone when it 
hides important aspects of performance. 
Your analysis must include: 
 A clear comparison of the baseline and trained models. 
 Neural network training and validation curves. 
 Error analysis or inspection of representative successes and failures. 
 Discussion of overfitting, underfitting, and generalization. 
 Interpretation of model behavior using suitable methods. 
 Limitations and realistic next steps. 
The neural network does not need to outperform the other models. A well-supported explanation of the results 
is more important than achieving the highest score.

6 Make the workflow reproducible 
Submit code that another person can use to reproduce your workflow. Include: 
 A README with setup and execution instructions. 
 Required packages and versions. 
 Dataset access instructions. 
 Random seeds where applicable. 
 Clearly organized preprocessing, training, and evaluation code. 
Demonstrate how the selected model generates a prediction or other relevant output for a new example. A 
deployed application is optional. 
Deliverables 
 Code: Organized scripts or notebooks, environment requirements, and a README. 
 Presentation and defense: A concise explanation of your project, supported by results and a demonstration 
of the working pipeline. 
Cite datasets, external code, pretrained models, and other sources. Disclose any AI assistance and ensure you 
can explain all submitted work. 
Project defense 
During the defense no phones in hand are allowed, be prepared to: 
 Explain your complete ML workflow. 
 Justify your preprocessing, model choices, and evaluation metrics. 
 Describe how your neural network learns. 
 Explain how you prevented data leakage and evaluated generalization. 
 Interpret your results, including unexpected findings and unsuccessful experiments. 
 Walk through key parts of your code and demonstrate your solution. 
For group projects, every member must explain their contribution and demonstrate an understanding of the 
overall project. 
Grading rubric 
Component Weight 
Problem formulation and motivation 10% 
Data understanding and exploratory analysis 15% 
Preprocessing and evaluation design 15% 
Model implementation, neural network, and tuning 30% 
Results, interpretation, and critical analysis 20% 
Presentation and defense 10% 
Total 100% 
Grades will reflect the quality of your methodology, reasoning, implementation, and communication, not 
model performance alone.
